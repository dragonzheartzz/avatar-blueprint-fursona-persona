## Context and objective

Tilly requested a consolidated assessment of the avatar discovery/assembly service after reviewing three independent reports separately: Pheme Social & Consumer Signals, Pheme Research Architect & Market Intelligence, and an Astro Teller-inspired adversarial challenge. Tilly clarified that the service should be understood as a holistic persona-to-blueprint offer with an optional, separately quoted route into the company's full avatar-production service.

## Consolidated outcome and evidence

- There is credible qualitative evidence of friction around asset discovery, compatibility, cost, and the technical gap between kitbashing and a finished avatar. The reports do not establish market size or willingness to pay for our proposed blueprint.
- BOOTHPLORER, Yorimichi Closet and VRCFinder already offer meaningful discovery features, mostly centred on BOOTH. Basic search is not a sufficient differentiator. The proposed cross-source, persona-led synthesis may help, but actual source coverage and performance have not been measured in a hands-on timed comparison.
- Adjacent creators sometimes sell asset sourcing alongside implementation. This is not proof of demand for a standalone blueprint.
- The intended commercial offer is a complete, practical avatar blueprint that remains useful independently, with an optional separate quote for our avatar-production service. Its downstream customer-acquisition value is a hypothesis, not a demonstrated result.
- Price hypotheses (£5–£9 basic, £12–£20 detailed) remain unvalidated. Neither research time nor production cost has been measured. The anticipated DSH Web/MCP/CLI/Blender/Unity workflows have not been audited as a verified end-to-end capability in this review.
- The reports' concerns about free substitutes, support burden, repeat demand and labour economics remain material. Astro's high-confidence PARK/REJECT recommendation is not justified by the research evidence alone, because the broader blueprint-plus-production model was not directly tested.
- Rights caution is retained. Original meshes, textures and rigs may be part of a viable production approach, but creating a new file or making visual/rigging changes does not automatically clear rights where protected expression is copied. UK guidance: [copyright permissions](https://www.gov.uk/using-somebody-elses-intellectual-property/copyright), [copyright overview](https://www.gov.uk/copyright), and the [18 March 2026 UK Government Report on Copyright and AI](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/report-and-impact-assessment-on-copyright-and-artificial-intelligence/report-on-copyright-and-artificial-intelligence). This is not legal advice.
- The full reasoning, report-by-report synthesis, and bounded requirements for the next stage are recorded in section 14 of the canonical Research Log and section 15 of the Service Brief.

## Recommendation

**Metis recommendation: ADVANCE to a bounded concept-planning/specification stage with Athena.**

Reason: the problem is credible enough and the refined proposition sufficiently distinct from a basic search directory to justify requirements work. The evidence does not justify an immediate rejection, but it also does not justify implementation, spending, customer testing, integration, pricing approval or launch.

The planning stage should specify the initial customer segment, guided intake paths, blueprint structure, supported source coverage, confidence and licence rules, transparent optional-production pathway, delivery economics, actual production hand-off requirements, and the smallest decisive fail-first test.
