# AI Avatar Discovery and Assembly Service

**Created:** 27 October 2026  
**Status:** Initial specialist review complete; Metis recommends advancing to bounded Athena concept planning/specification; CEO decision remains final; no build or launch authorised  
**Document type:** Product/service concept and research brief  
**Canonical workspace:** Tuesday Company Market Research  
**Related initiative:** Existing auto-avatar production project, whose exact repository and current interface must be confirmed before integration planning.

## 1. Executive concept

Offer a low-cost, independent service that turns a customer's avatar persona or persona specification sheet into a practical, researched plan for making that avatar.

The customer describes the character they want, including appearance, style, platform, budget and preferences. The service researches relevant avatar bases and assets across available marketplaces and creator sites, then returns a structured list of what is needed, what can be bought or obtained for free, what appears compatible, what remains uncertain, and what must be custom-made or modified.

The customer pays for the preparation and discovery work, not for a mandatory commission. They can take the resulting specification to any creator, assemble the avatar themselves, or choose our separate avatar production service.

The product is **not** intended to become a centralised marketplace or to require creators to list their assets on a new platform. The initial value proposition is an intelligent research and orchestration service across a fragmented ecosystem.

## 2. Customer promise

**Working message to test:**

> Tell us the avatar you want. We'll research the pieces, check what works together, estimate the cost and give you a plan you can use yourself or take to any creator.

The output should be useful even if the customer never purchases production from us. Independence and portability are core trust principles, not a temporary lead-generation trick.

## 3. Proposed customer journey

1. **Submit a persona brief.** Customer completes a structured form or provides an existing persona/specification sheet, plus platform, budget, desired style, must-have features and constraints.
2. **Clarify the brief.** Identify missing information, conflicts, non-negotiable requirements and any ambiguity that could materially affect results.
3. **Research assets.** Search relevant marketplaces, creator catalogues and other permitted sources for base avatars, bodies, heads, hair, clothing, accessories, props, textures and related assets.
4. **Assess fit and dependencies.** Record compatibility evidence, supported avatar base/version, technical dependencies, licence terms, modification requirements and confidence. Do not present a text-match as confirmed technical compatibility.
5. **Identify the gaps.** Distinguish assets that can be found from those that need fitting, editing, retopology, rigging, Blender work, texture work or creation from scratch.
6. **Produce the discovery blueprint.** Give the customer source links, free and paid alternatives, current listed prices where available, expected total asset spend, caveats and a prioritised assembly plan.
7. **Offer optional production.** Explain that we can separately quote for custom creation, fitting, repair, assembly and preparation for the target platform. The customer is free to take the report elsewhere.
8. **Capture learning.** With appropriate consent, record time spent, missing sources, compatibility failures, customer satisfaction and whether the customer would pay for this result again.

## 4. What the paid deliverable contains

The report should be structured and portable, not only conversational prose. A first version should contain:

- A concise interpretation of the requested persona and the assumptions used.
- One recommended direction plus meaningful alternatives where possible.
- Base avatar options and the required component list.
- Direct links to the original asset/creator listings.
- Free and paid alternatives, clearly labelled.
- Listed prices and a total estimated asset budget, with a last-checked date.
- Compatibility status for each component: confirmed from source, supported by evidence, plausible but unverified, or incompatible.
- Dependencies and technical requirements that may affect assembly.
- Licence, attribution, commercial-use, modification and redistribution checks, including any unresolved questions.
- A gap analysis: assets found, assets needing adjustment, and assets that appear to require custom creation.
- A production outline identifying possible Blender, Unity or other tool work, without promising that an untested automation pipeline can complete it.
- A clear list of remaining uncertainties and recommended next steps.

The report should never imply that the customer has acquired an asset licence simply because it appears in the report. Purchases, downloads, modifications and uploads remain subject to the creator's terms and the customer's permissions.

## 5. Customer segments to test

Do not assume a single audience before evidence is collected. Initial candidates:

- **New or non-technical avatar users:** know the character they want but do not know which assets or workflow to choose.
- **Persona/fursona-led hobbyists:** have a detailed character concept and want a closer match than an off-the-shelf avatar provides.
- **Existing avatar owners:** want to change their style or assemble a coordinated outfit and accessories for a known base.
- **Creators and commissioners:** want to reduce time spent sourcing assets and preparing customer options.

A narrow first test should choose one segment and one target ecosystem, likely VRChat given the initial research focus. Other avatar systems should be added only after their catalogues, formats and compatibility rules have been separately assessed.

## 6. Initial competitor and alternative landscape

The first sweep on 27 October 2026 identified several adjacent offerings. These are starting points for a deeper, source-linked competitor review, not a claim that the market has been exhaustively mapped.

| Service / alternative | Observed position in initial sweep | Implication to test |
|---|---|---|
| BOOTHPLORER | BOOTH-focused asset search and catalogue discovery, with filtering and compatibility-oriented features | Basic catalogue search is not a differentiator on its own. |
| Yorimichi Closet | VRChat/BOOTH item discovery with avatar compatibility and outfit/closet features | Compatibility and coordinated looks are already being addressed. |
| VRCFinder | Search for VRChat-related BOOTH assets by categories and attributes | We need to test whether persona interpretation and end-to-end planning add value beyond filters. |
| AllVRC | Directory of avatars, bases, clothing, accessories, tools and related resources | Directory aggregation exists; assess coverage and whether it produces an actionable build plan. |
| baseworks | Custom avatar personalisation workflow combining asset selection, fitting and implementation | Investigate whether standalone paid discovery is available or underserved compared with bundled customisation. |
| Individual creators on VGen and similar commission platforms | Some creator offers include asset sourcing, estimated costs and subsequent customisation/assembly | The research/preparation stage has recognisable value, but independent willingness to pay remains unproven. |
| VRCommissions and other commission directories | Connect customers with artists and technical specialists | We should sell a bounded lower-cost deliverable, not compete directly with full commissions or create another artist marketplace. |
| Official VRChat avatar marketplace and general tutorials | In-platform discovery, eligible avatar purchases and self-service learning | Establish what users can already do easily and where external asset workflows remain difficult. |

**Competitive conclusion so far:** the idea is not novel merely because it searches for avatar assets or checks outfit compatibility. The candidate differentiation is a persona-led, cross-source, portable blueprint that covers the whole desired look, budget, dependencies, compatibility confidence and custom-work gaps, with no requirement to buy the finished avatar from us.

The next sweep must verify each competitor's current features, prices, terms, target user and actual output using a common test brief. The exact live source URLs and evidence excerpts should be recorded before relying on specific feature or market-coverage claims.

## 7. Pricing and revenue hypotheses

The following are **test prices, not established market rates**:

- **Basic discovery report: £5–£9.** One bounded persona brief, concise asset shortlist and a basic cost/requirements summary.
- **Detailed assembly blueprint: £12–£20.** Broader alternatives, compatibility/dependency checks, licence notes, estimated budget and custom-work gap analysis.
- **Optional production quote:** separately priced according to asset creation, fitting, repair, assembly, validation and platform preparation required.

The objective is to undercut conventional custom-avatar services substantially for the discovery stage while keeping the scope precise. The service must not promise unlimited research for a token fee. A report that consumes hours of manual work may not be economically viable at the proposed prices.

Alternative models to compare, not assume: free initial shortlist plus paid detailed plan; fixed-fee report; optional paid human review; or creator-facing/B2B research assistance. Do not start with a subscription unless usage frequency and customer behaviour support one.

## 8. Delivery economics and evidence to collect

For each test brief, record:

- Total research and report-production time.
- Time spent per source and asset category.
- Number of relevant candidates found and the proportion with verifiable compatibility.
- Number of gaps requiring custom creation or modification.
- How often listed prices, availability or licences could not be verified.
- Customer-rated usefulness and whether the report changed their plan.
- Whether the customer would pay the proposed price, distinguished from polite interest.
- Whether they choose to commission production, while preserving the report's standalone value.
- Likely support, correction and refund burden.

The first manual test should deliberately include a realistic, moderately complex persona. Do not optimise only for an easy example that hides the hardest parts of the service.

## 9. Relationship to the existing auto-avatar production project

The discovery service and the existing auto-avatar project are related but separate.

**Discovery service:** turns the persona brief into a researched, customer-owned plan. It can be delivered manually at first and must stand alone.

**Production project:** the existing early-stage project explores using MCP with Unity to assess, repair and prepare avatars for upload, with broader avatar and asset creation/assembly ambitions. Its current capabilities, architecture, tests and intended interfaces must be inspected before proposing any hand-off.

Potential later connection:
1. Discovery report contains a structured component manifest and production requirements.
2. Customer explicitly chooses our production service and approves the selected assets and scope.
3. A validated adapter translates the approved manifest into inputs the production project can actually consume.
4. The production pipeline assesses feasibility, identifies unsupported steps and performs only supported, authorised operations.
5. The customer receives the finished deliverable or a transparent report of incomplete steps.

Do not assume the production repository can already consume a persona sheet or asset manifest. Confirm the exact repository, current implementation status, test evidence, asset input formats and MCP-to-Unity interface. Avoid coupling the initial service to a capability that is still experimental.

## 10. Legal, rights, safety and trust requirements

- Respect marketplace terms, access policies, robots rules where applicable, documented APIs and rate limits.
- Do not assume unrestricted scraping, bulk downloading, automated purchasing or account actions are permitted.
- Link to the original creator/listing and preserve attribution.
- Check asset licences for personal use, commercial use, modifications, derivative works, redistribution and combination with other assets.
- Do not bundle, redistribute or modify third-party assets without clear rights and permissions.
- Treat price, availability and licence information as time-sensitive; record the source and last-checked date.
- Label compatibility evidence and uncertainty. A visually similar item is not proof that it fits a particular rig, base, body, version or Unity project.
- Do not claim legal advice or guarantee that an asset can be used in every context.
- Keep customer persona information limited to what is needed for the service and explain how it will be used.
- Do not purchase assets, contact creators, negotiate access, upload customer files or perform production work without the relevant specific authorisation.

## 11. Research plan and decision gates

### Gate A: Competitor and source map
Verify current discovery tools, creator services, marketplaces, supported ecosystems, access methods, pricing and licence constraints. Use a common persona brief to compare actual outputs.

### Gate B: Customer problem and willingness to pay
Research discussions and customer experiences from the selected audience. Look for evidence of people struggling with sourcing, compatibility, budgeting and commission preparation. Distinguish complaints from evidence of purchase intent.

### Gate C: Manual delivery trial
Prepare a small number of reports using a repeatable checklist. Measure labour, completeness, compatibility confidence and customer response. Any real participant recruitment or handling of customer data should have an appropriate consent and privacy approach.

### Gate D: Unit economics and offer
Compare observed delivery cost with the proposed fee. Narrow the report scope, change pricing, or stop if the service cannot be delivered reliably and economically.

### Gate E: Production hand-off assessment
Inspect the auto-avatar repository separately and document the actual integration boundary. This does not block a standalone discovery trial.

At each gate, recommend **INVESTIGATE**, **VALIDATE**, **WATCH/PARK** or **STOP** based on evidence. A favourable concept review is not proof of demand.

## 15. Refined business framing and stage recommendation (28 October 2026)

### 15.1 Current proposition

The customer-facing offer is a **holistic Avatar Blueprint**, not merely a discovery report or search directory. Its purpose is to help a customer move from a desired persona/fursona or an existing character specification to a coherent, practical route towards the complete avatar.

The guided intake should eventually distinguish relevant starting paths, including humanoid and anthropomorphic avatars, a customer who already has a base/avatar, and a customer beginning from a broad concept. The output should bring together the likely whole-look component plan, suitable source links, estimated costs, compatibility evidence and uncertainty, dependencies, usage/licence notes, gaps requiring adaptation or custom work, and next steps.

Customers may use the blueprint independently, take it to any creator, or request a separate quote for our avatar-production service. The production option is a legitimate, transparent continuation of the customer journey and a potential source of additional work; it must not make the standalone blueprint incomplete or misleading.

### 15.2 Commercial role and differentiation hypothesis

The intended role is twofold:
1. Provide a useful, affordable blueprint as an independent product/service.
2. Where appropriate, create a fair route for customers to discover and choose our competitively priced full avatar-creation service.

The hypothesised differentiation is persona-led whole-look synthesis across relevant sources, cost and dependency visibility, compatibility confidence, explicit remaining-work gaps, and portability. Basic search, filters and links already exist for free. Cross-source comprehensiveness is a hypothesis to verify, not a market fact.

Initial price ranges (£5–£9 basic and £12–£20 detailed) remain hypotheses. Automated research/production using DSH Web, MCP/CLI, Blender and Unity may improve costs, but no measured delivery economics or complete production interface has been verified here.

### 15.3 Outcome of the three-perspective review

The 28 October review considered Pheme's Social & Consumer Signals report, Pheme's Research Architect & Market Intelligence report, Astro Teller-inspired challenge, prior source-backed research, and Tilly's clarification of the whole-persona blueprint and optional production pathway.

- Pheme's consumer report supports real qualitative discovery and compatibility friction, but not the size of the buyer segment or standalone willingness to pay.
- Pheme's market report accurately highlights capable free substitutes, adjacent creator services, unvalidated pricing and the need to measure delivery time. It evaluates the narrower report product more directly than the full blueprint-plus-production model.
- Astro's challenge correctly identifies the danger of selling information when customers want an outcome, and the risks from free substitutes, labour, support and weak differentiation. Its high-confidence PARK/REJECT recommendation is not established by the evidence provided.
- The three reports together support continued, bounded concept work; they do not justify implementation, pricing approval or launch.

See the full consolidated evidence and reasoning in the [Research Log, section 14](AI-Avatar-Discovery-Research-Log.md).

### 15.4 IP and asset creation boundary

Original assets created for the customer's brief could be a useful production path and may reduce dependence on third-party component purchases. However, a new Blender mesh, different rig, changed topology or visual alteration does not automatically establish that an asset is legally independent of a specific protected work. Use a design process based on the customer's requested attributes and independently created expression; record provenance and rights; use third-party assets only under applicable permissions; and escalate close-replica requests rather than relying on “different enough” as a legal test. See [GOV.UK copyright guidance](https://www.gov.uk/using-somebody-elses-intellectual-property/copyright) and [GOV.UK copyright overview](https://www.gov.uk/copyright). This is a risk to design around, not by itself a reason to stop the concept.
